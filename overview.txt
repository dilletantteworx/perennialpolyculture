# YIELD ESTIMATOR: PERENNIAL POLYCULTURE

## OVERVIEW

Mechanistic yield model for perennial polyculture guilds using linear algebra.
Estimates yield at arbitrary locations and is consistent with known science for monocultures and other types of intercropping and permaculture style guilds.

Location is characterized by two vectors:

## Location Vector (L)

Static characteristics: Annual rainfall (mm), rainfall seasonality, depth to bedrock (m), 
soil parent material, baseline soil texture (% sand, silt, clay), temperature profile 
(mean annual, growing season, min winter), last spring frost date (median ± σ), 
first fall frost date (median ± σ), historical freeze event frequency and severity (°C), 
mid-winter thaw frequency (events/decade), elevation (m), slope and aspect (°).

## Dynamic Location Vector (A)

Soil composition, soil temperature (under canopy), water table depth, etc.

## Plants have:
  - Requirement vectors (must fit current L and A)
  - Nutrition vectors for each salient growing season period
  - Canopy tier (direct sun) or groundcover tier (least sun)

Future: Carbon capture monetization for un-harvested biomass.

## GUILD ASSEMBLY

1. Choose anchor and secondary species such that no pair blooms coincidentally
2. Add flowers: satisfy pollinator/parasitoid calendar and density.
3. Add nitrogen fixers and soil nutrition specialists: balance nutrition, achieve ground cover, prevent weeds.
4. Check root penetration depths vary adequately; avoid waterlogging.
5. Check yield against historical pricing.

## RESOURCE FUNCTIONS

Form: f(resource) ∈ [0, 1]
  - Below threshold: negligible growth
  - Linear ramp: threshold to near-maximal range
  - Maximal range: Approximate as 1
  - Toxicity range: slope or cutoff to zero

Data source: Published plant vectors and resource response functions.
Substitution: Use closely related species if direct data unavailable.

## f(POLLINATORS, PARASITOIDS) — MINIMAL GUILD APPROXIMATION

### Operational Thresholds

**Nectar Continuity**
  nectar_continuous = (max_gap ≤ 7 days) during main flowering season

**Spatial Density**
  density_adequate = (mean_distance ≤ 10 m) between nectar sources

**Bloom Calendar**
  Extension phenology data ± 1 week; cultivar-specific windows.

**Nectar Supply** (optional refinement)
  Nectar(t) = SUM(flowers/plant × nectar/flower/day)
  where nectar/flower varies 2–5x with temperature, soil moisture, N.

### Parasitoid Suppression

f(pests) = exp(−α·I[nectar_continuous] − β·I[density_adequate] − γ·H)

where:
  I[·]           = indicator (1 if true, 0 if false)
  H              = Shannon diversity index (guild species)
  α, β           ≈ 0.3–0.5 (backward-fitted from field data)
  γ              ≈ 0.1–0.2 (diversity penalty coefficient)
  Uncertainty:   ±20–30% on α, β, γ

### Combined Statement

**Primary form:**
f(pollinators, parasitoids) = (1 − f(pests)) × I[nectar_continuous] × I[density_adequate]

**Alternative form** (penalty for unmet thresholds):
f(pollinators, parasitoids) = 
  {  (1 − f(pests))          if nectar_continuous AND density_adequate
  {  (1 − 0.5·f(pests))      otherwise

### Interpretation

- Thresholds met: pollinator visits adequate; parasitoid suppression active.
- Thresholds unmet: suppression reduced or absent.
- Pesticide and disease are separate: f(disease), f(frost).

### Data Uncertainty

Bloom timing:              ±1–2 weeks (phenology model error)
Nectar per flower:         2–5x range (T, moisture, N sensitivity)
Flowers per plant:         Cultivar/weather-dependent; rarely published
Pollinator demand:         Crop-specific literature; guild-context untested
Mitigation:               User-reported bloom dates & pest counts refine parameters;
                          high-quality reporters eligible for co-authorship.

## f(DISEASE)

**Form:**
f(disease) = baseline × [1 − (c_local × H_guild) − (c_landscape × H_landscape)]

where:
  baseline                = species- and location-specific baseline pressure
  H_guild                 = Shannon diversity within 3–5 species guild (~1.0–1.6)
  H_landscape             = surrounding ecosystem diversity (0 = barren, 1 = unmanaged forest)
  c_local                 ≈ 0.10–0.15 (within-guild suppression)
  c_landscape             ≈ 0.05–0.10 (landscape dilution benefit)

**Baseline Determination:**
  - Parameterize from location climate (spring temperature, rainfall frequency, leaf-wetness hours).
  - Regional extension pathology records or literature tables by species/zone.
  - Alternative: Location dynamic vector (A) includes humidity; use max expected humidity + 20% margin
    and select cultivars tolerant to that threshold; bypass explicit humidity calculation.

**NOTE:** Canopy humidity effect is embedded in baseline; separate calculation deferred to v2 (thinner understory modeling).

## f(FROST): Phenological Frost Damage

## Abstract Definition

f(frost) = ∏_i [1 − L_i(T_min, T_hardy_i, W_soil, Y_loss_i)]
  (independent per-crop loss terms, no synchrony penalty)

where phase ∈ {spring, fall, thaw}

## Severity Function (Abstraction)

Define buffering factor:
  B(W_soil) = 1 − β_w × (W_soil / W_FC)
  
where β_w is soil-dependent moisture buffering coefficient, W_FC is field capacity.

Define conditional loss:
  L_sev(T_min, T_hardy, W_soil, Y_loss) = 
    0                           if T_min > T_hardy
    Y_loss × B(W_soil)          if T_min ≤ T_hardy

## Phenophase Loss Terms

L_spring = P(frost | bloom) × L_sev(T_min, T_bloom_hardy, W_soil, Y_loss_bloom) × (1 − R_secondary)

L_fall = P(frost | ripen) × L_sev(T_min, T_fruit_hardy, W_soil, Y_loss_fruit)

L_thaw = (f_thaw / 10) × P(bud_burst_early) × δ_thaw

where:
  P(frost | phase) = probability frost occurs during phenophase (from historical records + GDD)
  T_bloom_hardy, T_fruit_hardy = species cold hardiness thresholds by phenophase
  Y_loss_bloom, Y_loss_fruit = species yield-loss-per-event parameters (0–1)
  R_secondary = secondary bloom probability (0–1)
  f_thaw = mid-winter thaw events per decade (location vector)
  P(bud_burst_early) = species-specific probability of false bud break during thaw
  δ_thaw = yield loss per thaw event (0–1)

All temperature, probability, and yield terms are species and location attributes; 
B(W_soil) couples the function to dynamic soil moisture.

# f(temperature): Synergistic Temperature–Water Response

## Abstraction

Define optimal temperature response (independent):
  f_T(T) = piecewise linear ramp [T_min_opt, T_opt_low] → [1, 1] → [T_opt_high, T_max_opt]
  
Define transpirational cooling effect:
  f_W(W, T) = 1 − γ_cool × max(0, T − T_baseline) × (1 − W_soil / W_FC)

## Combined Function

f(temperature) = f_T(T) × f_W(W, T)

where:
  T = growing season mean or instantaneous temperature (context-dependent)
  W_soil, W_FC = current and field-capacity soil moisture
  γ_cool = transpiration effectiveness coefficient (species and climate dependent)
  T_baseline = reference temperature above which cooling is needed

Interpretation: Temperature stress is reduced by soil moisture availability; wet soil 
allows transpirational cooling, dry soil amplifies heat stress. Below T_min_opt or 
above T_max_opt, both terms approach zero independently; synergy occurs in mid-range.

# f(water): Soil Moisture Resource Function

## Definition

f(water) = piecewise linear:
  0                           if W_soil < W_crit_low
  (W_soil − W_crit_low) / W_range    if W_crit_low ≤ W_soil ≤ W_opt
  1 − α_sat × (W_soil − W_opt)²      if W_opt < W_soil ≤ W_FC
  penaltyₛₐₜ                   if W_soil > W_FC

where:
  W_soil = current volumetric soil moisture (%)
  W_crit_low, W_opt = species-specific critical and optimal moisture thresholds
  W_range = W_opt − W_crit_low
  α_sat = saturation penalty curvature (species dependent)
  penaltyₛₐₜ = baseline penalty for waterlogging (e.g., 0.3–0.7, species/soil dependent)

Interpretation: Below critical threshold, no growth; linear ramp to optimum; 
quadratic decline above optimum; cliff drop into saturation.

---

# Coupling: f(temperature) ↔ f(water) ↔ f(frost_freeze_events)

Water availability buffers temperature extremes via latent heat (modeled in f_W(W,T) above).
Soil moisture also reduces frost severity (via B(W_soil) in frost function).
In dry conditions, both heat and cold stress increase synergistically.

Implementation: Compute f(water) first; pass W_soil to f(temperature) and f(frost_freeze_events).

## YIELD FORMULA

Yield = [f(water) × f(sunlight) × f(temperature) × f(soil_structure)
         × f(N) × f(P) × f(K) × f(pH) × f(micronutrients)
         × (1 − f(pests)) × (1 − f(disease))
         × f(pollinators, parasitoids)
         × (1 − f(frost_freeze_events))
         × f(labor_management)]
        × [potential_yield_for_species]
